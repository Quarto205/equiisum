SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE; TABLE OF CONTENTS.
This Act may be cited as the Clean Energy Innovation and Grid Resiliency Act of 2026.
SEC. 2. FINDINGS AND PURPOSE.
Congress finds that modernizing the electrical transmission infrastructure is vital to national security and economic growth.
The purpose of this Act is to accelerate federal permitting for high-voltage interstate transmission lines and provide low-interest loans for clean grid projects.
SEC. 3. NATIONAL GRID RELIABILITY INITIATIVE.
The Secretary of Energy shall establish a competitive grant program to allocate $2.5 billion in funding for advanced energy storage systems.
Eligible entities include regional transmission organizations, independent system operators, electric cooperatives, and municipal utilities.
Grants awarded under this section shall not exceed 50% of the total cost of any single capital project.
SEC. 4. INTERCONNECTION QUEUE STREAMLINING.
The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission shall promulgate rules requiring transmission providers to adopt cluster study methodologies for generator interconnection requests.
Transmission providers shall complete system impact studies within 180 days of queue closure.
Failure to meet statutory deadlines without good cause shall result in administrative penalties of up to $50,000 per business day.
SEC. 5. CRITICAL MINERAL SUPPLY SECURITY.
The Director of the United States Geological Survey shall update the national critical minerals inventory biannually.
There is authorized to be appropriated $150 million to support domestic processing technologies for lithium, cobalt, and rare earth elements.
Projects receiving financial support shall comply with prevailing wage requirements under subchapter IV of chapter 31 of title 40, United States Code.
SEC. 6. WORKFORCE TRANSITION ASSISTANCE.
The Secretary of Labor shall establish training and apprenticeship programs in communities affected by the closure of fossil fuel power generating facilities.
Appropriations of $300 million are authorized for fiscal years 2027 through 2031 to fund technical certifications in wind, solar, and battery storage installation.
Priority in awarding training grants shall be given to veterans and historically underrepresented groups in technical trades.
SEC. 7. CYBERSECURITY ENHANCEMENTS FOR BULK POWER SYSTEMS.
The Secretary of Homeland Security, in coordination with the National Institute of Standards and Technology, shall publish comprehensive zero-trust network guidelines for electric grid operators within 270 days.
Electric utilities serving more than 100,000 customers must certify compliance with national cyber defense standards annually.
An emergency reserve fund of $500 million is authorized to assist rural cooperatives with immediate malware mitigation and ransomware incident recovery.
SEC. 8. INTERAGENCY TASK FORCE ON PERMITTING COORDINATION.
There is established an Interagency Task Force comprised of representatives from the Department of the Interior, the Environmental Protection Agency, and the Army Corps of Engineers.
The Task Force shall develop unified environmental review schedules for linear energy infrastructure crossing federal lands.
Environmental assessments shall not exceed 12 months in duration unless an extension is specifically approved by the President.
SEC. 9. SEVERABILITY AND SAVINGS CLAUSE.
If any provision of this Act or the application thereof to any person or circumstance is held invalid, the remainder of the Act shall not be affected thereby.
Nothing in this Act shall be construed to diminish the statutory authority of state public utility commissions regarding retail electric rate structures.
SEC. 10. REPORT TO CONGRESS.
Not later than two years after the date of enactment of this Act, the Comptroller General of the United States shall submit to the Committee on Energy and Natural Resources a comprehensive performance audit of all grant allocations made under this Act.
